State Space Models for Astrophysics

Supervised by Alain Celisse · SAMM Lab, Paris 1 · 2026, ongoing

1 · Why variable stars?

A variable star is a star whose apparent brightness varies measurably over time. The underlying mechanisms are physically distinct: sustained radial pulsations, mutual eclipses within a binary system, geometric modulations, and each imprints a distinctive signature on the photometry. These variations are among the few observables that directly constrain the internal structure and evolutionary state of a star. The elementary object of study is the light curve: a time series of triplets (t, m, σ), Julian date, apparent magnitude, photometric uncertainty, at irregular sampling.

The data. OGLE-IV (Optical Gravitational Lensing Experiment, fourth phase; Udalski et al., 2015) is a ground-based photometric survey conducted since 2010 with the 1.3 m telescope at Las Campanas (Chile), which notably monitors the Magellanic Clouds in the I and V bands. Its derived catalog, the OGLE Collection of Variable Stars (OCVS), provides for each star an expert classification, a reference period, and the complete photometry. The present work focuses on a subset of about 150,000 stars spread across six classes: classical Cepheids (cep), anomalous Cepheids (acep), type II Cepheids (t2cep), RR Lyrae (rrlyr), Delta Scuti (dsct), and eclipsing binaries (ecl).

2 · The six classes

Once folded on its period (§3), each class displays a characteristic morphology, a direct trace of the physical mechanism driving the variability. The six OCVS classes fall into two families: five classes of pulsators, and eclipsing binaries, where the variation is purely geometric.

cep

Classical Cepheids

Massive, young stars undergoing radial pulsation, sustained by the κ mechanism. Periods from ~1 to more than 100 days; typical fast-rise, slow-decline asymmetry.

acep

Anomalous Cepheids

Same pulsation mechanism, but in lower-mass stars whose position in the instability strip is explained by a binary origin (merger or mass transfer). Periods from ~0.4 to 2.5 days.

t2cep

Type II Cepheids

Evolved radial pulsators of low mass and old population. Three subclasses by increasing period: BL Her (1–4 d), W Vir (4–20 d), RV Tau (>20 d, alternating minima).

rrlyr

RR Lyrae

Old, metal-poor radial pulsators, on the horizontal branch. Periods from ~0.2 to 1 day; RRab (fundamental mode, asymmetric curve) and RRc (first overtone, near-sinusoidal curve) subtypes.

dsct

Delta Scuti

A/F-type stars near the main sequence, periods below ~0.3 day. Pulsation is frequently multimode, combining radial and non-radial modes (cf. figure 3).

ecl

Eclipsing binaries

Variability of geometric origin: mutual eclipses of two orbiting stars. Morphology governed by the system's configuration (detached, semi-detached, contact) and orbital inclination.

3 · Phase folding

For a signal of known period P, each observation date reduces to a phase φ = ((t − t₀) / P) mod 1 ∈ [0, 1), where t₀ is a reference epoch. This reparametrization superimposes all cycles onto a single interval: this is phase folding. Several hundred noisy measurements, scattered over a decade, condense into an estimate of the average shape of the cycle, the operation that produces the morphologies of figure 2.

The quality of the folding depends entirely on that of the period. An incorrect period does not produce a noisy version of the true shape: it superimposes unrelated portions of the cycle, destroying the structure rather than revealing it. Figure 4 illustrates the typical error regimes: sub-multiple, correct period, multiple.

The operation nevertheless has a structural limitation: folding estimates a mean over cycles. Any cycle-to-cycle variability: amplitude modulation, alternating minima, multimode beating, is eliminated by construction. This loss of information motivates the approach developed from §5 onward.

4 · Feature-based classification: classical parameter spaces

Beyond morphological inspection, traditional classification relies on a small number of scalar descriptors extracted from the folded curve: period, amplitude, Fourier coefficients, in which classes occupy distinct and physically interpretable regions. Three projection planes summarize the bulk of this discriminative power.

These descriptors: period, amplitude, truncated Fourier coefficients, are hand-crafted features. Discriminative and interpretable, they nonetheless rest on strong structural assumptions: a single period, a strictly repeated shape from one cycle to the next, a number of harmonics fixed a priori. The following sections examine what these assumptions exclude, and how to move beyond them.

5 · Limitations of the folding approach

Two distinct limitations motivate abandoning the folding-features pipeline in favor of a representation learned directly on the raw sequence.

Loss of cycle-to-cycle information. The RV Tau panel of figure 2 provides the canonical example: deep and secondary minima alternate from one cycle to the next. Once the curve is folded on its fundamental period, and even more so once summarized into period, amplitude, and tabulated Fourier coefficients, this alternation disappears entirely. The estimated mean is correct, but any systematic deviation from that mean (alternation, amplitude modulation, multimode beating of figure 3) is eliminated by construction, consistent with the strictly repeated shape assumption identified in §4.

Sampling structure. The sampling function of a ground-based survey is strongly bimodal (figure 8): since the target is only observable part of the year, an intra-season cadence of a few days alternates with inter-season gaps of several months. Any method operating on the raw sequence must represent this temporal information explicitly, treating it as a plain sequence of values indexed by rank amounts to conflating a 2-day gap with a 120-day gap.

These two limitations point to the same alternative: directly encode the raw sequence (t, m, σ) and let the model handle irregular sampling itself. This is precisely what the HiPPO → S4 → Mamba lineage enables: a recurrent memory derived from a continuous-time ODE, whose dynamics depend explicitly on the physical time elapsed between two measurements, not on the sequence rank alone. The following sections develop this approach.

6 · State space models: the HiPPO → S4 → Mamba lineage

The underlying problem is that of any recurrent architecture: compressing a past of arbitrary length into a fixed-size state. Structured state space models give a continuous-time formulation of this. A SSM passes an input signal \(u(t) \in \mathbb{R}\) through a latent state \(x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N\) according to the linear system

$$ x'(t) = A\,x(t) + B\,u(t), \qquad y(t) = C\,x(t), \tag{1} $$

where \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}\) governs the state dynamics, and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}\), \(C \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}\) are the input and output projections; in practice, such a system is applied independently to each channel of a representation of dimension \(D\).

HiPPO. The choice of \(A\) is not arbitrary. The HiPPO framework (Gu et al., 2020) seeks matrices \(A\) such that the state \(x(t)\) maintains, at every instant, the coefficients of the optimal projection of the history \(u_{\le t}\) onto a basis of orthogonal polynomials (Legendre for HiPPO-LegS). The system's memory is thus derived rather than learned, and defined natively in continuous time: the starting point from which everything else follows.

S4: discretization and duality. To apply (1) to a sampled sequence, the continuous system is discretized with a step \(\Delta > 0\) using zero-order hold:

$$ \bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}\left(\exp(\Delta A) - I\right)\Delta B, \tag{2} $$

which gives the linear recurrence

$$ x_k = \bar{A}\,x_{k-1} + \bar{B}\,u_k, \qquad y_k = C\,x_k. \tag{3} $$

The contribution of S4 (Gu, Goel & Ré, 2022) is to make this system trainable at scale: a stable parametrization of \(A\) (initialized from HiPPO), and exploitation of the same model's dual reading. Unrolled, the recurrence (3) is equivalent to a convolution with the kernel

$$ \bar{K} = \left(C\bar{B},\; C\bar{A}\bar{B},\; \dots,\; C\bar{A}^{L-1}\bar{B}\right), \qquad y = u * \bar{K}, \tag{4} $$

computable in \(O(L \log L)\) over a sequence of length \(L\): the recurrence is used at inference (constant cost per measurement), the convolution at training time (parallel over the whole sequence). The model's limitation is its LTI character: \((\bar{A}, \bar{B}, C)\) are fixed, the same dynamics apply to every input.

Mamba: selection. The limitation of LTI is that a single filter applies to the entire sequence: the model cannot choose to keep an important measurement while discarding another. Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) lifts this constraint by making the parameters content-dependent: each input \(u_k\) determines, through learned linear projections \(s_\Delta, s_B, s_C\), the parameters used at that specific step:

$$ \Delta_k = \operatorname{softplus}\!\left(s_\Delta(u_k)\right), \qquad B_k = s_B(u_k), \qquad C_k = s_C(u_k). \tag{5} $$

The discretization (2) is then recomputed at every step: \(\bar{A}_k = \exp(\Delta_k A)\). The interpretation is direct. A large \(\Delta_k\) drives the state to evolve rapidly, old memory fades, and the current measurement dominates. A small \(\Delta_k\) freezes the state: the current measurement is nearly ignored. \(\Delta_k\) thus acts as an internal, content-driven clock: the model tunes, measurement by measurement, the balance between remembering and forgetting.

This flexibility comes at a cost: since the parameters vary along the sequence, the recurrence (3) is no longer equivalent to a fixed convolution, and the parallel training route via (4) is no longer available. Mamba replaces it with a parallel scan; the recurrence is associative, and thus computable in parallel by blocks, implemented close to the GPU's fast memory. Mamba-2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) closes the loop by showing, via state space duality, that these selective recurrences and the attention mechanism are two instances of the same family of transformations.

The connection to §5 lies in equation (2): \(\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A)\) depends explicitly on the time step, and nothing forces \(\Delta\) to be constant. By substituting for the learned \(\Delta_k\) (5), or combining with them, the actual gaps \(\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1}\) of the OGLE sampling, an inter-season gap of 120 days translates into a long evolution of the state, quantitatively distinct from an intra-season gap of 2 days, whereas any rank-indexed sequence model conflates the two.

7 · Method: representation and training objectives

Input. The model operates on the raw curve of §1, without folding or feature extraction. Each star is represented by the sequence

$$ u_k = \left(\Delta t_k,\; \tilde{m}_k,\; \sigma_k\right), \qquad k = 1, \dots, L, \tag{6} $$

where \(\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1}\) is the time gap from the previous measurement, \(\tilde{m}_k\) the per-star normalized magnitude, and \(\sigma_k\) the photometric uncertainty. No label is provided: neither period nor Fourier coefficients, everything the descriptors of §4 encode must be learned by the model, including what folding eliminates (§5).

Encoder. A Mamba backbone processes this sequence and produces a representation \(z = f_\theta(u) \in \mathbb{R}^d\), on which two complementary objectives are defined.

Classification objective. A linear head predicts one of the six OCVS classes via cross-entropy:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ce}} = -\log p_\theta\!\left(y \mid z\right), \tag{7} $$

where \(y\) is the class label. This objective provides the direct point of comparison with the literature (§8), but only constrains the decision boundary: two very different representations can produce the same classifier.

Shape-supervised contrastive objective. A triplet loss structures the latent space beyond class boundaries. For an anchor \(z_a\), a positive \(z_p\), and a negative \(z_n\),

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tri}} = \max\!\left(0,\; \left\lVert z_a - z_p \right\rVert^2 - \left\lVert z_a - z_n \right\rVert^2 + \alpha\right), \tag{8} $$

with \(\alpha > 0\) the margin. The decisive point is how pairs are chosen: positives and negatives are not defined solely by the class label, but by the DTW distance between folded curves, the positive is a curve with low DTW distance to the anchor, the negative one with high distance. Folding, whose limitations as a representation were established in §5, is thus redeployed as a similarity supervisor: the notion of proximity injected into (8) is a physically meaningful shape similarity, not a data-augmentation artifact.

The total objective combines both terms,

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ce}} + \lambda\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tri}}, \tag{9} $$

where \(\lambda > 0\) sets the relative weight of the contrastive structure.

The division of labor between (7) and (8) is deliberate: classification ensures that the latent space separates the classes, the contrastive term ensures that it organizes them. Two curves of the same class but distinct morphologies, an RRab and an RRc, a BL Her and an RV Tau, should occupy different regions of the latent space, without any subclass label ever being provided to the model. §8 quantifies this property precisely.

8 · Evaluation: beyond accuracy, the geometry of the latent space

The point of comparison is set by recent literature on light curve classification, in particular multimodal approaches such as AstroM³, with a quantitative target: exceeding 91.5 % accuracy on the six OGLE-IV classes for the Large Magellanic Cloud. Accuracy, however, remains a poor summary of what a model has learned: two models with the same score can produce radically different internal representations. Evaluation therefore focuses, beyond the score, on three properties of the latent space.

Alignment and uniformity. Wang & Isola (2020) decompose the quality of a contrastive representation, normalized on the unit sphere, into two quantities. Alignment measures the closeness of positive pairs,

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}} = \mathbb{E}_{(x, x^{+}) \sim p_{\mathrm{pos}}} \left\lVert f(x) - f(x^{+}) \right\rVert^2, \tag{10} $$

and uniformity the degree to which the set of representations occupies the sphere,

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{unif}} = \log \, \mathbb{E}_{x, x' \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}} \exp\!\left(-2 \left\lVert f(x) - f(x') \right\rVert^2\right). \tag{11} $$

A good representation minimizes both: similar pairs close together (10), without the whole set collapsing onto a few directions (11). In our setting, \(p_{\mathrm{pos}}\) is defined by the DTW similarity of §7, and both metrics directly evaluate what objective (8) was meant to produce.

Neighborhood consistency. For each star, the fraction of its \(k\) nearest neighbors in the latent space sharing its class, subclass, or period range. This local diagnostic complements the two global quantities (10)–(11): it detects regions where the geometry is locally inconsistent even when the aggregate statistics are good.

Emergence of subclasses. The most demanding test: projecting the latent space into two dimensions (UMAP) and examining whether the subtypes of §2: RRab/RRc, BL Her/W Vir/RV Tau, contact/detached binaries, form distinct regions, even though no subclass label was provided during training. An emergent separation would indicate that the representation captures structure finer than the supervision received: the latent geometry would reflect the underlying physics, rather than the taxonomy imposed alone.

This is the question that motivates the project beyond the score: to what extent does the physical structure of a dynamical system: pulsation modes, period, orbital configuration, show up in the geometry of the representation learned by a sequential architecture? Variable stars are a privileged testbed: the physical ground truth is known, catalogued, and independent of the model being evaluated.
